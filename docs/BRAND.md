**Read this before writing any copy, any product page, any landing section.**

## 1. Who we actually are

We are a **workshop**, not a shop.

We own the machines. We make things — 3D printed, machined, hand-finished — **piece by piece**. Two ways to buy from us:

| | |
|---|---|
| **The Shelf** | Pieces we have already made. Finite by nature — we made four, there are four. |
| **The Workshop** | You describe it, we make it. Print-on-demand and made-to-order. |

Everything else in this document follows from one fact: **we are the origin of the object, not a step in its distribution.**

---

## 2. The market truth that creates our opening

Moroccan online retail is overwhelmingly **arbitrage**. Someone buys a container of generic goods, photographs them, and resells. The photo on the listing is a stock photo of a factory sample. Nobody knows who made anything.

This produces three chronic wounds:

1. **Nobody can answer "who made this?"** — because the seller doesn't know either.
2. **Quality is a lottery** — the box rarely matches the listing.
3. **Trust is broken, which is why COD exists at all** — the customer refuses to pay until the object is physically in their hands.

Every competitor shares these wounds because every competitor has the same structure. **We do not have that structure.** That is the entire opportunity.

## 3. Position

> ### Made, not resold.
> **نصنع، لا نعيد البيع**

Supporting line:
> **Everything else online came from a container. Ours came from a workshop.**
> **كل شيء آخر جاء من حاوية. أما قطعتنا فجاءت من ورشة.**

For The Workshop offer:
> **If we don't have it, we make it.**
> **إن لم نجده لك، صنعناه لك.**

### Why this line and not another

It is a **spear aimed at a weakness the competition structurally cannot fix.** A reseller can copy our photos, our prices, our page layout, our ad copy — all of it, in an afternoon. They cannot copy a workshop. To match this claim they would have to stop being a reseller and become a manufacturer.

That is the definition of a moat: an advantage that costs the competitor more to copy than it is worth to them.

## 5. The flywheel

This is where the business and the software become the same thing.

```
        make a small batch
                ↓
     story-rich page, real photos
                ↓
   feed engine watches what earns attention
                ↓
      make more of what wins · stop what loses
                ↓
   almost no dead capital · higher margin
                ↓
        more capacity to make
                ↓
        (back to the top, wider)
```

**The feed engine in `REBUILD-PLAN.md` is not a recommendation feature. It is the workshop's demand sensor.** It answers the only question that matters to a maker: *what should I make next?*

This is Zara's model — read demand fast, produce in small runs, never guess — run by one workshop instead of a global supply chain. A reseller cannot operate this loop; their inventory was committed months ago, in a container, before anyone clicked anything.

## 6. The COD economics nobody talks about

In cash-on-delivery markets, **15–30% of packages are refused at the door.** A refused package costs shipping in both directions and returns zero revenue. It is the single largest silent cost in Moroccan e-commerce.

Refusals happen for one reason: **the box surprised the customer.**

So the AI concierge is not a novelty. It is a refusal-reduction machine, and it is how it pays for itself:

- Every question answered *before* purchase is a refusal that never happens
- Real photos of the real piece — no stock images, ever
- Exact dimensions, weight, material, finish, stated plainly
- Honest, specific timelines — "ready in 6 days," never "soon"
- Confirm the order out loud before placing it

**Target: drive refusals below 8%.** That difference is not a marketing win. It is margin, and it compounds every single month.

## 7. Strategies we are borrowing, and exactly what we take

| Company | What they proved | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| **Everlane** | Radical cost transparency builds fierce trust | Show material cost, machine hours, finishing time on the page |
| **Tesla** | Build-to-order beats warehouse guessing | No inventory gamble. The Workshop is our default, not our exception. |
| **Zara** | Small runs + fast demand reading | Make 5, watch the feed, make 50 — or stop |
| **Nike By You** | Customization commands a premium | Customization costs a reseller a fortune. It costs us nothing — it's how we already work. Charge accordingly. |
| **In-N-Out / Trader Joe's** | A small menu increases confidence | A short catalogue is a feature. "We make few things. We make them properly." |
| **Patagonia** | Objects with origin stories outsell specs | Every product page tells how it was made, not what it weighs |
| **Apple** | The unboxing is part of the product | In COD, the doorstep *is* the brand moment. Packaging + a handwritten note. |
| **Rolex / Hermès** | Real scarcity is honest scarcity | "3 left" because we made 3. Never a fake countdown timer. |
| **Toyota (lean)** | Build after the order, not before | Negative working capital: no cash trapped in stock |

The user's own insight — *too many items = confusion = no purchase* — is already validated by In-N-Out and Trader Joe's. A curated catalogue and a feed that narrows itself are the same strategy expressed twice.

## 8. Proof mechanics — the tactics that make the claim real

A claim without proof is advertising. These make it evidence.

- **A face and a name.** In a market of anonymous accounts, being an identifiable human is itself a moat.
- **Piece numbering.** "Piece 04 of 12." Honest, and it makes the object particular rather than generic.
- **Process footage.** A few seconds of the machine or the hand-finishing on every product page.
- **The real photo rule.** The image is the actual piece being sold. This is non-negotiable and it is our loudest differentiator.
- **Handwritten card in every box.** Costs pennies. Returns disproportionate trust.
- **Honest, specific timelines.** "Ready in 6 days." Precision reads as competence; vagueness reads as evasion.
- **Show the workshop.** Not a polished studio — the actual bench, actual dust.

## 9. Voice

**Soft. Warm. Certain.** We are quietly confident because we made the thing.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "We made this one in October." | "PREMIUM QUALITY!!! 🔥🔥" |
| "Ready in six days." | "Fast shipping!" |
| "Three left — we made three." | "ONLY 3 LEFT! HURRY!" |
| "Tell us what you need." | "Contact us for inquiries." |
| Short sentences. Plain words. | Marketing throat-clearing. |

Arabic is written as Arabic — warm, direct, native. It is never a translation of English word order. Both languages carry the same calm.

---

## 10. What we never do

- Never a stock photo. Never a supplier image.
- Never a fake countdown or invented scarcity.
- Never "premium," "luxury," or "high-quality" as a claim — show it instead.
- Never compete on price against imported goods. That race is unwinnable and it is the wrong race.
- Never a vague delivery date.
- Never let a customer be surprised by the box.
- Never overwhelm the visitor with everything at once.

## 11. The one-paragraph story

> Almost everything sold online in Morocco arrived in a container. Someone photographed it, listed it, and shipped it on. Nobody in that chain has ever touched the thing they're selling.

>
> And if what you want isn't there, tell us. We'll make it.
>
> **Made, not resold.**
